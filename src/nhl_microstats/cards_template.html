Microstat Cards

Ranked against every qualified skater at their position. A custom xG model scores every shot; Edge numbers come from NHL puck and player tracking.

90th+67–89th 33–66thbelow 33rd Tick = league median. A higher percentile is always better: for stats where fewer is better (penalties taken, goals and xG against on/off ice, beaten on goals against), fewer ranks higher.

Rush: the puck was recorded outside the attacking zone in the previous 8 s with no faceoff since. The NHL logs no zone entries, so play-by-play only catches about 6% of attempts as rush (video tracking finds 20–30%). Rush percentiles rest on small counts. Cycle: 10+ s of in-zone play since the last exit or faceoff, not a rebound or rush.

Most on-ice results (shots, xG and goals for and against while he's on the ice) are left off, with four exceptions. xGF% (the headline number) and Fenwick %: his team's share of xG and of unblocked shot attempts while he's on the ice. Goals against on/off ice %: goals against per 60 with him on the ice as a % of his team's goals against per 60 with him off it, in the same games; 100 means no different and lower is better. Goals are rare, so it swings a lot over a few games. xG against on/off ice %: the same for xG against, at 5v5 in both views; it settles much faster than the goals version. Takeaways: scorers started logging far fewer takeaways in 2024-25, so takeaways rank each season against its own season, and a season in progress against the latest complete one. Penalties: minors and majors, not fighting majors or misconducts; fights are counted on their own under Physicality.

Quality of competition: the 5v5 xGF% of the opposing skaters he was on the ice against, weighted by the 5v5 time they shared (each opponent at his actual season xGF%). Higher means tougher opponents; it's 5v5 in both views. Most players land within a point or two of 50%, so small gaps move the percentile a lot.

Categories: each section's stat percentiles are averaged ("avg"), and that average is then ranked like any other stat: every qualified player-season in the comparison pool gets the same average, and a logistic curve fitted to those averages (forwards and defense separately) turns his into a percentile. An average of percentiles bunches toward the middle, so an average of 70 can be a 90th-percentile category. Overall does the same one level up: a weighted average of his category percentiles, ranked by a curve fitted to every pool player-season's weighted average. Forwards: Offence, Finishing and Play driving ×3, Defence ×2, Penalties ×1, Speed and Physicality ×½. Defensemen: Play driving and Defence ×3, Offence ×2, Finishing and Penalties ×1, Speed, Physicality and Competition ×½. Competition (who he played against) doesn't count for forwards.

Not available from NHL data: passes, shot assists, zone entries and exits, entry denials, retrievals.

Goal tracking: from the NHL's puck and player tracking of the ~14 s before each goal, the data behind nhl.com's goal replays. It only exists for goals, so these count what a player did in the build-up to his team's goals: time with the puck, carries over the offensive blue line, defenders beaten (an opponent within 10 ft who was ahead of the carrier and ends up behind him), dekes (the puck swinging 2.5+ ft across his body within 0.5 s with an opponent or the goalie within 10 ft; not checked against video). On the other side: beaten on goals against. Possession is inferred from the puck staying within 5 ft of a skater and moving with him. The NHL has this tracking from 2023-24 on.
